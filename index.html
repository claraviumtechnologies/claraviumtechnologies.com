02

The shelf

A bank or fund platform offers hundreds of funds.

Most come from outside managers. Each fund carries a prospectus, a KID, an SFDR annex and periodic reports.

  • 100s funds on a typical shelf
  • 1,000s documents behind them

03

Every distributor’s problem

Distributors must stand behind every fund they offer.

Today their own criteria (exclusions, sustainability minimums, naming rules) are checked against fund documents by hand.

Checking fund documents is manual today, so it is hard to show from evidence why each fund is on the shelf.

The gap today

04

The hidden problem

The documents themselves can’t be trusted.

It surfaces only at marketing review, in distributor due diligence, or when the regulator asks.

Stale

KIDs and fund pages go out of date and keep old costs and risk scores.

Contradictory

Costs, risk scores and holdings differ between prospectus, KID, SFDR annex and reports.

05

Why now

Distributors must keep reviewing every fund they offer.

MiFID II product governance and ESMA’s 2023 guidelines require regular checks that each fund still fits its target market. Today that review is largely manual.

Sources: MiFID II, Directive 2014/65/EU, Art. 16(3) and 24(2); ESMA Guidelines on MiFID II product governance requirements (ESMA35-43-3448, 2023).

Every fund. Every review cycle.

MiFID II · ESMA 2023

06

The product

Fund Anvil: cited evidence on every fund.

  • Reads every fund’s full public document set.
  • Checks it against the distributor’s own criteria.
  • Flags stale and contradictory documents.
  • Cites the page behind every finding.
  • In the app, by API, or through MCP in your own AI tools.

Fund Examination covers return, risk, cost, flows, holdings as published, gaps and limits. A continuous monitor for funds on a distributor’s shelf, and for managers who want their own published record inspected privately.

Disclosure Review compares the documents against each other and flags each inconsistency with a severity and the effort to resolve it. An evidence layer beside counsel, not in place of it.

Fund Examination

What one examination covers.

Six sections, with every fund compared side by side. Figures from a test on one fund manager’s public documents.

Return

Each fund against cash and against its own benchmark, year by year.

Risk

One fund: deepest loss −56%, worst calendar year −53%, 23 months to recover.

Cost

One fund: a 1.15% ongoing charge, but 2.42% actually charged in 2025.

Money leaving

Four funds lost 28% of value to redemptions in a year they returned +3% to +32%.

Holdings

Not one individual holding named in any audited annual report.

What is published

Which figures each fund publishes, and which none of its documents state.

09

Alternatives

Data and process are covered. Evidence is not.

Fund data hubs

FE fundinfo and Morningstar store and distribute documents and data, not per-finding evidence.

Product-governance workflow

Ruleguard and Deloitte OneView organise reviews. They track the process, not what is inside the documents.

Ratings and general AI

Ratings terminals score funds. General AI summarises without findings you can cite.

Manual review

Spreadsheets are slow, uneven and hard to evidence.

The cited consistency layer between those tools: findings anchored to the issuer’s own files, with severity and effort to resolve, as a continuous monitor.

Evidence, not advice.
